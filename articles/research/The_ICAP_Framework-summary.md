## ICAP framework — summary

**Definition**
The ICAP framework, developed by Michelene T. H. Chi, classifies learning activities by the *depth of cognitive engagement* they produce.

**Core idea**
Learning improves not with activity alone, but with how much the learner *transforms* information.

---

## The four modes (low → high impact)

* **Passive** — receiving information (reading, listening)
  → minimal learning gains

* **Active** — manipulating information (highlighting, copying)
  → modest gains; often superficial

* **Constructive** — generating new understanding (explaining, summarizing)
  → strong gains; real comprehension begins

* **Interactive** — co-constructing knowledge with others (dialogue, critique, refinement)
  → highest gains when both parties contribute meaningfully

---

## Key distinction

* Passive and Active = **engagement without transformation**
* Constructive and Interactive = **engagement with transformation**

This is the hinge of the framework.

---

## Why it matters

ICAP provides a practical diagnostic:

* It separates *visible activity* from *actual learning*
* It predicts which learning designs will produce durable understanding
* It exposes why many “engaging” experiences underperform

## Practical takeaway

If you want better learning outcomes:

* Don’t optimize for activity
* Design for **generation (constructive)** and **co-construction (interactive)**

Everything else is lower-yield, regardless of how busy it looks.
